# Broader history and a continuous visitor journey

Research and local implementation reviewed November 3, 2026. This is a coverage audit and next research programme, not a claim that the full era has been documented.

## What is missing

The existing 47 credited entries are not 47 developed artist histories. Many came from one publication's credits. The 26 moments strongly privilege Songcamp, Sound and Music Nerd's immediate orbit. The list needs better coverage and deeper stories, not an arbitrary larger count.

- **Origins and distinctions:** blockchain payment/licensing experiments, social tokens, redeemable tokens and music NFTs are related but different. Avoid declaring a single first music NFT without a definition and contemporary evidence.
- **Parallel scenes:** headline celebrity drops, independent one-of-ones, low-cost editions, royalties, audiovisual/generative works and open collaborative releases developed alongside one another.
- **Geography and access:** other chains, Latin American and non-English-language communities, fees, invitations, collecting budgets and technical barriers.
- **The lived experience:** who introduced whom; where listening and feedback happened; organizers, visual artists, developers, collectors and writers; tensions and disagreement as well as creative successes.
- **Consequences:** releases and repeat collaborations, income claims with denominators, work that continued outside crypto, failed promises, platform dependence and preservation.
- **The present:** dated artist and platform trajectories. A closure, acquisition, maintenance mode, unavailable domain and an abandoned token are different states. A July 2025 ecosystem map is not November 2026 truth.
- **Artifacts:** fewer generic portraits reused across moments; more actual covers, mint pages, dated flyers, public room screenshots, demos, process clips and participant audio, with rights and provenance recorded.

## How to research deeply enough

1. Build a coverage matrix by period, geography, format, platform and role. Audit gaps before expanding any one familiar community.
2. For each candidate moment, record event date, publication date, original URL and archived capture if available; the exact supported claim; named participants/roles; artifact; uncertainty; rights/credit for imagery. Use existing fixture/source structures, not a new knowledge backend.
3. Follow artist trajectories across platforms: first known experiment → introductions → creative output → later work. Require credit, participant account or another specific source for relationships. Shared ownership or a shared event does not establish collaboration.
4. Use Pete's public wallet only to discover release/contract leads. Verify artist identity, original release terms and chain before adding them. Document network coverage and collection date; do not infer friendship or endorsement from holdings. The previous partial Ethereum pass is insufficient.
5. Inspect public original mint/release pages, artist blogs/newsletters, dated event programmes, interviews and podcasts. Preserve timecodes for audiovisual testimony. A dead URL should trigger an archive/source search, not an invented date or automatic shutdown label.
6. Prepare oral histories around concrete artifacts: How did you first hear about it? Who introduced you? What did you make? What did collectors receive? What was difficult? Who is missing from this account? Where is that work now? No outreach has been sent.
7. Separate contemporaneous evidence, later recollection and editorial interpretation. Keep contradictory accounts side by side until resolved. Use secondary mapping to discover leads and test selection bias.
8. Give every platform a dated lifecycle record: launch, important changes, accessibility, current statement, actual observed access and preservation limitations. Recheck at publication, especially claims drawn from 2025 maps.
9. Select representative stories once evidence is adequate. A strong main scroll should take roughly 6–8 minutes; optional chapters can go deeper. Breadth belongs in the research and branches, not an endless wall of names.

## Proposed larger narrative

Before the collectible (rights and payments) → the public boom and independent experiments → rooms and relationships → new ways to make together → tensions and unequal outcomes → platforms change → what artists carried forward.

Each act needs one concrete artifact, one human story, an explanation of what changed, optional listening, and a supported connection to another act. The current local scroll is a working interaction slice using already sourced material; it does not yet implement all of this historical coverage.

## Experience change implemented now

The homepage now proceeds directly into five illustrated sequences / ten short passages before the optional timeline index. No click is needed to begin or continue. Desktop images stay alongside the text; on phone the image and text share native document flow. Project art transitions to the three Walls contributors as their roles become the subject. Artist/source sheets retain place; audio is explicit and stoppable. Reduced motion removes transformations. No wheel interception, forced snap, automatic sound or delayed text reveal.

The current five repeated image/text structures are a functional first pass, not the final editorial rhythm.
