## Paper edit / whole-path assessment

1. Establish the musical stakes: support for making work, relationships with listeners, collective creation. Preview funding, video and CHAOS as different experiments, not a universal program. Use actual CHAOS artwork rather than a virtual-room image unrelated to the opening individual.
2. Earlier roots: Imogen Heap's licensing/payment experiment; RAC/Zora's redeemable cassette; Catalog and its founders. Explain why each changes the question, and distinguish earlier blockchain work from NFT editions.
3. Encounter: Songcamp's calls and the human effort behind them. Follow Three Oscillators into a collaboration rather than listing participants. Future Surf shows the routine needed to sustain a scene; it is dated 2023, not presented as preceding Sound's launch.
4. Release: explicitly rewind to 2021. Daniel Allan, Sound and Soulection demonstrate funding, editions and shared proceeds. These are different mechanisms, not successive replacements.
5. Collective: CHAOS's scale becomes intelligible through Walls and its three credited makers. The later remix follows a credit, not an invented causal relationship.
6. Belonging: LATASHA's account supplies tension between access promised by technology and actual exclusion. Teaching and gatherings are actions in response.
7. Afterlives: website closure and royalty dependence test the promise; The Park demonstrates ongoing care. Pete's builder perspective is attributed, not assigned to everyone.

The six movements remain thematic rather than a strictly linear chronology. This pass replaces the opening and chapter orientation; it does not claim a complete documentary rewrite or exhaustive era coverage. Musicben remains an accessible participant/source, not the movement's originator. Jah remains discoverable context, not a separate main chapter.
